Part B — Safety, machines and results

5. Industrial safety

Important: Zagel Vision is an inspection and automation tool. It is not a safety-rated control system, safety function, emergency stop, light curtain, machine guard, interlock or substitute for a properly designed, validated and maintained safety system.

Never use the Software as the only means of protecting people, machinery, products or the environment. Safety functions must be provided by independent, appropriately rated hardware and controls that work even when this Software is stopped, disconnected, faulty or misconfigured.

6. PLC, machine and action control

The Software can send configured commands to PLCs and other equipment, including commands started by automatic actions, product results, alarms, remote messages or manual tests. It can also read signals from them, such as start and stop requests, sensor triggers, counter resets, batch numbers and reject confirmations. Incorrect configuration, network faults, delays, software errors or unexpected equipment behavior could cause damage or injury.

  • Have qualified personnel review and validate each connection, address, operation, value, timing and action before enabling it.
  • Commission and test changes in a safe environment, and watch the equipment when a change is first used in production.
  • Keep independent machine guards, interlocks, emergency stops and safety-rated controls in place and tested.
  • Use test and manual controls only when the machine and work area are in a safe condition for the commanded output.
  • Plan for failure: define the safe state of every output when the Software, network or a camera fails, including any fail-safe or fallback behavior you configure.
  • Do not enable remote start, stop, reset or reject commands from external systems unless you have assessed who can send them and what they can cause.

You are responsible for the risk assessment, design, commissioning, operation, maintenance and safe configuration of the complete machine and control system, and for compliance with applicable machine-safety rules and standards.

7. Inspection results and AI output

Detection, classification, tracking, counting, barcode or QR reading, product verdicts and other results can be inaccurate, incomplete, delayed or unavailable. Results depend on camera position, lighting, focus, speed, product variation, training data, model choice, thresholds and configuration. They are decision support and must be validated for your real operating conditions.

  • Test each model and rule with representative data before relying on it in production, and again after any change to the product, lighting, camera, model or settings.
  • Monitor performance continuously and set procedures for missed detections, false detections, uncertain results and faults.
  • Keep a way to check or recover products that may have been missed, wrongly rejected or wrongly accepted.
  • Do not use the Software as the sole basis for decisions that could cause serious harm, such as releasing food, medicine, medical or safety-critical products, unless you have independently validated the full process.

8. Lines, cameras and joined results

A production line can have several cameras. Cameras may work as independent stations or may join one product result, matched by travel time or sequence. Counting direction, tracking, trigger source, reject timing and matching rules are configured by you. Wrong settings can cause products to be counted twice, missed, matched to the wrong result or rejected incorrectly. You are responsible for choosing, testing and reviewing these settings for each line.
